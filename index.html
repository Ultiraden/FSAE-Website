Team History

Over a decade of Highlander Racing.

2024

HR24-E

Highlander Racing's most recent electric vehicle. This car is the culmination of 2 years of work and the first one since 2023. We were able to compete in 3 events and placed 61/100 overall in the 2025 FSAE EV Competition. A highlight is that this was the first year we ever competed in the Cost Scenario, gaining 29 points from our 2023 score, and placed 41/76.

2023

HR23-E

This car was a completely new design, along with a full team reorganization. Brought in more EE and CS team members to improve our EV capabilities. With this new team composition, we were able to develop smarter, faster, and more efficiently. We competed in 3 events and placed 53/61 overall.

2020

HR20-E

New design with new accumulator packaging, increased system voltage, improved suspension and chassis packaging, and corner assemblies with smaller unsprung mass.

2019

HR19-E

The first full electric competition car with All Wheel Drive and hub motor design, torque vectoring, and an in-house motor, aero package, and battery assembly.

2018

HR18-C

Our last internal consumption car, and we participated in 6 events, placing 49/67. We unfortunately seized our engine in the acceleration event, but were able to swap the entire engine in 4 hours. Unfortunately, our rear brake system failed during endurance, but we still were able to finish top 50 overall. Some highlights are that we passed tech inspection on the first try.

2015

HR15-C

Known as the “Bytes Car”, it's currently displayed in Chung Hall’s Bytes. This car participated in 4 events and ended up placing 59/65.

2009

HR09-C

This was our first Formula car, with all previous ones being Baja style. We had a team of 13 students, but we were still able to bring a functioning car to the competition. While we were not able to compete in all events, we made it through tech inspection in one go and completed the endurance event.
